Do AI apps actually work? The research, read honestly: meta-analyses of computer-assisted pronunciation training find a real, medium-sized effect (d ≈ 0.68; ASR-based training g ≈ 0.69), strongest for individual sounds (g ≈ 0.82) and when the app gives explicit corrective feedback (g ≈ 0.86). Three catches. First, the effect on intonation, stress, and rhythm, the delivery half, is less than half as strong (g ≈ 0.37). Second, gains shrink for advanced speakers; most studies were run on beginner and intermediate students, not professionals. Third, under 4 weeks of use shows almost nothing (g ≈ 0.07); the effect needs months of short daily practice. Published efficacy studies are almost all on ELSA in classroom settings; BoldVoice has excellent user ratings and coaching content but no independent studies; Yoodli measures useful delivery proxies (fillers, pacing) but has no efficacy research at all. Verdict: worth it as a daily drill layer for sounds at 10–15 min/day sustained for months, and as a feedback mirror on real calls. Not a substitute for a human on delivery, structure, and executive presence.
Does 1:1 human coaching work? The parallel numbers, equally honestly. Human pronunciation instruction shows a larger headline effect than apps (d ≈ 0.80–0.89 across 86 studies), but with a serious catch: most of those studies measured careful read-aloud tasks, not real conversation, and reviews conclude efficacy for spontaneous speech "remains relatively unclear." The field's key controlled comparison found that only global instruction (prosody, fluency, discourse) improved how understandable speakers were in spontaneous speech; sound drilling alone did not transfer. On the delivery side, workplace-coaching meta-analyses find real but modest effects: δ ≈ 0.36 overall (skill outcomes δ ≈ 0.28, affective δ ≈ 0.51; RCTs g ≈ 0.43, strongest for behaviors at g ≈ 0.73), and nearly every outcome in that literature is self-reported. No study has ever compared apps head-to-head against 1:1 coaching, and combined engagements versus separate ones have never been studied. Verdict: humans do not beat apps on measured effect size; they beat them on what the measurements miss, transfer to real, spontaneous, high-stakes speech. The evidence-backed play is an app for the daily drill layer plus a human who rehearses your actual material.
